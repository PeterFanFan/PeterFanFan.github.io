Abstract

Sign language generation aims to translate spoken or written language into coherent sign motion sequences for accessible communication. Existing methods are largely based on Transformers or GANs and typically formulate sign language generation as a direct sequence-to-sequence mapping from linguistic input to skeletal motion. However, they often struggle to learn semantically meaningful and structurally robust motion representations, resulting in inaccurate hand trajectories and reduced sign intelligibility. We propose Sign-HADiff, a two-stage diffusion-based framework for sign language generation. In the first stage, HAUnet-Diffusion learns latent representations of sign motion via reconstruction-based diffusion modeling. In the second stage, a latent space predictor maps linguistic inputs into the sign latent space, which is further used to condition the diffusion model for sign sequence generation. By decoupling representation learning from conditional generation, our framework improves both semantic fidelity and motion quality. Our framework has two core designs. First, we introduce HAUnet, a Hierarchical Attention U-Net that enhances the DDPM backbone with Spatial Augmentation Attention and Hierarchical Feature Integration, enabling more effective multi-level spatial modeling and better preservation of fine-grained skeletal structure. Second, we develop GLSP with a Gloss Semantic Enhancer that combines regional and global semantic modeling to generate semantically enriched latent conditions. Extensive experiments on PHOENIX14T and How2Sign demonstrate that Sign-HADiff consistently outperforms competitive baselines on standard evaluation metrics.


Proposed Method

Overall framework of Sign-HADiff. GLSP predicts sign latent representations from linguistic inputs, and HAUnet-Diffusion then generates sign sequences conditioned on the predicted latent representations.


Structure of HAUnet-Diffusion. The framework consists of a Pose Pre-processing branch for encoding the sign sequence into a latent condition and a HAUnet-based diffusion branch for predicting the noise at diffusion step t.


Structure of HAUnet. The model enhances spatial representations at different downsampling levels through Spatial Augmentation Attention (SAA), and progressively integrates multi-level features by Hierarchical Feature Integration (HFI). The integrated features are then compressed by DimRed and fed to the decoder for diffusion-based noise prediction.
